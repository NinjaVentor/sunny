Understanding PNG to PDF

PNG is a lossless format, which makes it perfect for anything containing text, lines or flat colors — but many portals only accept PDF. The converter flattens each PNG onto a white A4 page (transparent areas become white, exactly as a printer would render them) and scales proportionally so nothing stretches.

For photographs, JPG to PDF usually gives smaller files; for screenshots and graphics, PNG to PDF keeps text razor-sharp. Mixing both? Convert each type with its dedicated tool, then join the results with PDF Merge.

Pro Tips for Best Results

  • Capture screenshots at full brightness for maximum text clarity.
  • Crop status bars and clutter before converting for tidier pages.
  • For long chat evidence, convert in chronological order.
  • Need it smaller? Lower PNG dimensions before conversion, not after.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to transparent backgrounds?
They are flattened onto white, the same way a printer handles transparency. Your content stays exactly where it was, on a clean page.
Is my screenshot data private?
Completely. Screenshots often contain personal chats or payment details — that is precisely why this tool processes everything locally and uploads nothing.
PNG or JPG for my document?
Screenshots, scans of text and graphics: PNG. Camera photos: JPG. This page handles PNG; photos go through JPG to PDF for smaller files.
Can I merge the result with other PDFs?
Yes. Download the PDF, then combine it with certificates or forms using our PDF Merge tool.
Why is my PDF bigger than expected?
PNG is lossless, so detailed screenshots carry more data. If a portal caps file size, compress the PNG first or convert photos to JPG instead.
