Understanding Age Calculator

Exact age is trickier than current-year minus birth-year: month lengths and leap years shift the answer. The calculator walks the calendar properly — borrowing months and days like long subtraction — so 29 February birthdays and year boundaries come out right.

Many official forms ask for age “as on” a specific date (admission cutoffs, retirement rules). Pick any reference date, not just today, to answer those questions precisely.

Save the result alongside the reference date whenever it matters: admission offices, HR departments and visa counters all interpret age relative to a cutoff, and a written record of “28 years, 4 months as on 31 March” prevents disputes that a bare birth year cannot settle. Recompute for each new cutoff rather than reusing old figures, since month boundaries shift the answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is exact age calculated?
By calendar walking: full years first, then remaining months, then days — correctly handling 28/29/30/31-day months and leap years.
What about 29 February birthdays?
Handled correctly: in non-leap years the anniversary lands per calendar rules, and day counts stay exact either way.
Can I check age on a past or future date?
Yes. Set any reference date — useful for school admission cutoffs (e.g. age as on 31 March) or retirement eligibility.
Is my birth date stored?
No. The calculation runs locally and nothing is sent or saved anywhere.
Why not just subtract the years?
Because birthdays later in the year make simple subtraction off by one — and forms often need months and days too.
