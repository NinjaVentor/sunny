Understanding PDF Split

A PDF is a collection of independent pages, so extraction is surgically clean: chosen pages are copied into a fresh file with their fonts and images intact. The result is a genuine PDF (selectable text included), not a photo of one — which is why split files remain suitable for official use.

Common flows: split out the signed page of a contract, separate a scanned batch into individual invoices, or pull your certificate out of a merged application file. Recombine selections later with PDF Merge.

Pro Tips for Best Results

  • Note the source page numbers before splitting large files.
  • Name outputs by content (Contract-Signed-Page.pdf), not split-1.pdf.
  • Delete the oversized original from shared drives after extracting.
  • Combine split + merge to reorder a document's pages.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does splitting lower quality?
Not at all. Pages are copied with all fonts and images intact — the output is a true PDF, identical in quality to the source pages.
How do I write a page range?
Use formats like 1-3 for a span, or comma lists like 1,4,7 for individual pages. The tool validates your input before splitting.
Can I split a scanned PDF?
Yes. Scanned pages split exactly like digital ones; the images inside are preserved at full resolution.
What about password-protected files?
Locked PDFs must be unlocked in your reader first — browsers cannot open encrypted files without the password.
Can I rejoin split files later?
Anytime. Our PDF Merge tool recombines split outputs in whatever order you choose.
