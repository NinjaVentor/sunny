Understanding Currency Converter

The converter fetches live reference (mid-market) rates — the fair midpoint between buy and sell prices. Exchange counters add their margin on top, so always compare their offer against this benchmark: the gap is your true transfer cost. Rates refresh automatically, and conversions compute instantly for any amount.

The dirham is pegged at 3.6725 per US dollar, so USD pairs barely move — while PKR, INR and PHP float daily, which is exactly where checking first pays off. For the full story on remittance math, read our guide to sending money home.

Pro Tips for Best Results

  • Check the rate before leaving for the exchange counter.
  • Compare total cost (rate + fees), not rate alone.
  • Screenshot the live rate when negotiating large transfers.
  • Read our remittance guide to understand margins.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this the rate I will get at the exchange?
Almost — this is the mid-market benchmark. Counters add a small margin, so expect a slightly lower figure. The benchmark tells you whether an offer is fair.
How often do rates update?
Rates refresh automatically in the background. For exact transfer timing, recheck just before you transact.
Why is USD to AED always ~3.67?
The dirham is officially pegged to the dollar at 3.6725, so that pair is stable by design. Other currencies float freely.
Can I convert any pair, like SAR to INR?
Yes — pick any two of the 30+ supported currencies; conversion routes through live cross-rates automatically.
Is the converter free?
Completely, with no account and no limits. Check rates every day if you like.
